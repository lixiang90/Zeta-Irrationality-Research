2.3. Special sum rules for $E_{4}$ and derivatives
Now, we evaluate $S_{r(z)}(z)$ [defined in (1.26)] and $T_{r(z)}(z)$ [defined in (1.27)] for some special points $z$ . In this process, we will repeatedly invoke the sum rules (2.27) and (2.50) for the Eichler integrals, as well as (tacitly) the translational invariance $E_{4}(z)=E_{4}(z+1)$ . These efforts will amount to the final proofs of (1.9)–(1.12) in Proposition 2.3 and refreshed views of (1.5)–(1.8) in Proposition 2.4.
Proposition 2.3.
(a)
We have
(2.55)
$\displaystyle E_{4}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)=\frac{2i}{\sqrt{3}}+\frac{30\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i},$
which entails
(2.56)
$\displaystyle S_{1/16}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)=\frac{11\pi^{2}}{96}.$
(b)
We have
(2.57)
$\displaystyle 12E_{4}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)-E_{4}\left(\sqrt{7}i\right)=\frac{29\sqrt{7}i}{6}+\frac{330\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i},$
which entails
(2.58)
$\displaystyle S_{1/46}\left(\frac{\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)=\frac{43\pi^{2}}{552}.$
(c)
We have
(2.59)
$\displaystyle 2E_{4}\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+E_{4}\left(\sqrt{2}i\right)=\frac{5i}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{90\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i},$
which entails
(2.60)
$\displaystyle S_{1/4}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right)=\frac{5\pi^{2}}{24}.$
(d)
We have
(2.61)
$\displaystyle E_{4}(i)=\frac{7i}{6}+\frac{30\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i},$
which entails
(2.62)
$\displaystyle S_{1/16}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\right)=\frac{11\pi^{2}}{96}.$
Proof.
(a)
To verify (2.55), plug $z=\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}$ into (2.27).
For any $z\in\mathfrak H$ , we have
(2.63)
$\displaystyle S_{1/16}(z)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{24}(\I z)^{2}+\frac{15\zeta(3)}{8\pi\I z}-\frac{\pi^{2}iE_{4}\big(z+\frac{1}{2}\big)}{16}.$
Specializing the equation above to $z=\frac{\sqrt{3}i}{2}$ , we get (2.56).
(b)
For $z=\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}$ , we have
(2.64)
$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{z}=\frac{1}{1-z},\quad-\frac{2}{z}=z-1,\quad-\frac{4}{z}=2z-2,$
so (2.50) brings us
(2.65)
$\displaystyle 0={}$
$\displaystyle 4E_{4}\left(\frac{1}{1-z}\right)+4E_{4}\left(-\frac{1}{z}\right)-9E_{4}(z)+E_{4}(2z).$
With the aid of (2.27), we may invert the arguments of the first two Eichler integrals on the right-hand side of the equation above, thereby confirming (2.57).
Here, the coefficient $12$ comes from the algebra
(2.66)
$\displaystyle\frac{4}{(1-z)^{2}}+\frac{4}{z^{2}}-9=-12$
for $z=\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}$ .
We have
(2.67)
$\displaystyle S_{1/46}(z)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{69}(\I z)^{2}+\frac{165\zeta(3)}{92\pi\I z}-\frac{\pi^{2}i\left[12E_{4}\big(z+\frac{1}{2}\big)-E_{4}(2z)\right]}{184}$
for any $z\in\mathfrak H$ . Specializing the equation above to $z=\frac{\sqrt{7}i}{2}$ , we get (2.58).
(c)
To verify (2.59), plug $z=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}$ into (2.27).
We have
(2.68)
$\displaystyle S_{1/4}(z)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}(\I z)^{2}+\frac{9\zeta(3)}{4\pi\I z}-\frac{\pi^{2}i\left[2E_{4}\big(z+\frac{1}{2}\big)+E_{4}(2z)\right]}{40}$
for any $z\in\mathfrak H$ . Specializing the last displayed equation to $z=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}$ , we get (2.60).
(d)
To verify (2.61), plug $z=i$ into (2.27).
Specializing (2.63) to $z=\frac{1}{2}+i$ , we get (2.62).
$\blacksquare$
Proposition 2.4.
(a)
We have
(2.69)
$\displaystyle E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)=-\frac{15\sqrt{3}L_{-3}(2)}{\pi^{2}i}-\sqrt{3}i,$
which entails
(2.70)
$\displaystyle T_{1/16}\left(\frac{\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)=\frac{\pi}{36}.$
(b)
We have
(2.71)
$\displaystyle 3E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)-E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\sqrt{7}i\right)=-\frac{35\sqrt{7}L_{-7}(2)}{4\pi^{2}i}-\sqrt{7}i,$
which entails
(2.72)
$\displaystyle T_{1/46}\left(\frac{\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)=\frac{\pi}{966}.$
(c)
We have
(2.73)
$\displaystyle E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+2E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\sqrt{2}i\right)=-\frac{40\sqrt{2}L_{-8}(2)}{\pi^{2}i}-5\sqrt{2}i,$
which entails
(2.74)
$\displaystyle T_{1/4}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right)=-\frac{\pi}{12}.$
(d)
We have
(2.75)
$\displaystyle E_{4}^{\prime\prime}(i)=-\frac{20L_{-4}(2)}{\pi^{2}i}-2i,$
which entails
(2.76)
$\displaystyle T_{1/16}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\right)=-\frac{\pi}{96}.$
Proof.
(a)
The second-order derivative of (2.27) reads
(2.77)
$\displaystyle E_{4}^{\prime\prime}(z)-\frac{1}{z^{2}}E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(-\frac{1}{z}\right)-2E_{4}\left(-\frac{1}{z}\right)-\frac{2}{z}E_{4}^{\prime}\left(-\frac{1}{z}\right)=-\frac{2}{3z^{3}}-2z-\frac{60\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i}$
for any $z\in\mathfrak H$ , where the integral representation of $E_{4}^{\prime}$ (resp. $E^{\prime\prime}_{4}$ ) is given by
(2.78)
$\displaystyle E^{\prime}_{4}(z)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\frac{\partial E_{4}(z)}{\partial z}=2\int_{z}^{i\infty}[1-E_{4}(w)](z-w)\D w$
[resp. (1.25)]. Setting $z=\frac{-1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}$ in the functional equation (2.77), we get
(2.79)
$\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{-1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)+\frac{1-\sqrt{3}i}{2}E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)-2E_{4}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)+\big(1+\sqrt{3}i\big)E_{4}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)\\
={}&\frac{1}{3}-\sqrt{3}i-\frac{60\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i},\end{split}$
whose imaginary part leaves us [cf. (′ ‣ (a))]
(2.80)
$\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{3}{2}E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)+\frac{\sqrt{3}i}{2}-\frac{60\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i}-\frac{2i}{\sqrt{3}}E\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2},2\right)=-\sqrt{3}i-\frac{60\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i}.\end{split}$
This is equivalent to (2.69), thanks to the closed-form evaluation $E\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2},2\right)=\frac{135L_{-3}(2)}{4\pi^{2}}$ (cf. Table 1).
Specializing
(2.81)
$\displaystyle T_{1/16}\left(z\right)=\frac{Q_{1}(z)-\frac{1}{16}Q_{2}(z)}{\pi(\I z)^{2}}-\frac{\pi i}{16\I z}E^{\prime\prime}_{4}\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)$
to $z=\frac{\sqrt{3}i}{2}$ , while recalling the proven evaluation $Q_{1}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{16}Q_{2}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{3}i}{2}\right)=\frac{11\pi^{2}}{96}-\frac{45L_{-3}(2)}{32}$ from the penultimate column of Table 1, we reach (2.70).
(b)
Twice differentiating (2.50) in $z$ , we may establish
(2.82)
$\displaystyle 0=E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)+E_{4}^{\prime\prime}(z)-9E_{4}^{\prime\prime}(2z)+4E_{4}^{\prime\prime}(4z)$
for any $z\in\mathfrak H$ . When $z=-\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{7}i}$ , this boils down to
(2.83)
$\displaystyle\begin{split}0={}&E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{4}\right)+E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{-1+\sqrt{7}i}{4}\right)-9E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{-1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)+4E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(-1+\sqrt{7}i\right)\\
={}&E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(-\frac{2}{-1+\sqrt{7}i}\right)+E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(-\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{7}i}\right)-9E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)+4E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\sqrt{7}i\right).\end{split}$
Applying (2.77) to $z=-\frac{2}{\pm 1+i\sqrt{7}}$ , we receive
(2.84)
$\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(-\frac{2}{\pm 1+\sqrt{7}i}\right)+\frac{3\mp\sqrt{7}i}{2}E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)\\
{}&-2E_{4}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)+\big(\!\pm 1+\sqrt{7}i\big)E_{4}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)\\
={}&\mp\frac{7}{6}-\frac{5\sqrt{7}i}{6}-\frac{60\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i}\end{split}$
in return. Extracting the imaginary part, we get
(2.85)
$\displaystyle\begin{split}&E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(-\frac{2}{-1+\sqrt{7}i}\right)+E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(-\frac{2}{1+\sqrt{7}i}\right)+3E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)\\
&{}-4E_{4}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)+2\sqrt{7}iE_{4}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)\\
={}&-\frac{5\sqrt{7}i}{3}-\frac{120\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i}.\end{split}$
Now that
(2.86)
$\displaystyle-4E_{4}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)+2\sqrt{7}iE_{4}^{\prime}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)=\frac{7\sqrt{7}i}{3}-\frac{120\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i}-\frac{4\sqrt{7}i}{3}E\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2},2\right)$
issues from (′ ‣ (a)), we arrive at (2.71) after recalling $E\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2},2\right)=\frac{105L_{-7}(2)}{4\pi^{2}}$ (cf. Table 1).
To show (2.72), simply study the $z=\frac{\sqrt{7}i}{2}$ case of
(2.87)
$\displaystyle T_{1/46}(z)=\frac{Q_{1}(z)-\frac{1}{46}Q_{2}(z)}{\pi(\I z)^{2}}-\frac{\pi i}{46\I z}\left[3E^{\prime\prime}_{4}\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)-E^{\prime\prime}_{4}(2z)\right]$
along with the proven identity $Q_{1}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)-\frac{1}{46}Q_{2}\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{7}i}{2}\right)=\frac{43\pi^{2}}{552}-\frac{245L_{-7}(2)}{368}$ (cf. Table 1).
(c)
With $z=\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}$ in (2.77), we are left with
(2.88)
$\displaystyle E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+2E_{4}^{\prime\prime}\left(\sqrt{2}i\right)-2E_{4}\left(\sqrt{2}i\right)+2\sqrt{2}iE_{4}^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{2}i\right)=-\frac{7\sqrt{2}i}{3}-\frac{60\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i},$
while
(2.89)
$\displaystyle-2E_{4}\left(\sqrt{2}i\right)+2\sqrt{2}iE_{4}^{\prime}\left(\sqrt{2}i\right)=\frac{8\sqrt{2}i}{3}-\frac{60\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i}+\frac{4\sqrt{2}E\big(\sqrt{2}i,2\big)}{3i}$
is a result of (′ ‣ (a)). Therefore, we may verify (2.73) through $E\big(\sqrt{2}i,2\big)=E\big(1+\sqrt{2}i,2\big)=\frac{30L_{-8}(2)}{\pi^{2}}$ (cf. Table 1).
The formula
(2.90)
$\displaystyle T_{1/4}(z)=\frac{Q_{1}(z)-\frac{1}{4}Q_{2}(z)}{\pi(\I z)^{2}}-\frac{\pi i}{20\I z}\left[E^{\prime\prime}_{4}\left(z+\frac{1}{2}\right)+2E^{\prime\prime}_{4}(2z)\right]$
becomes (2.74) when $z=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}$ , thanks to the proven evaluation $Q_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{4}Q_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{i}{\sqrt{2}}\right)=\frac{5\pi^{2}}{24}-2L_{-8}(2)$ (cf. Table 1).
(d)
Setting $z=i$ in (2.77), we find
(2.91)
$\displaystyle 2E^{\prime\prime}_{4}(i)-2E_{4}(i)+2iE_{4}^{\prime}(i)=-\frac{8i}{3}-\frac{60\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i},$
while
(2.92)
$\displaystyle-2E_{4}(i)+2iE_{4}^{\prime}(i)=\frac{4i}{3}-\frac{60\zeta(3)}{\pi^{3}i}+\frac{4E(i,2)}{3i}$
follows from (′ ‣ (a)). Therefore, we may confirm (2.75) right after invoking $E(i,2)=\frac{30L_{-4}(2)}{\pi^{2}}$ from Table 1.
Specializing (2.81) to $z=\frac{1}{2}+i$ , while looking up the entry $Q_{1}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\right)-\frac{1}{16}Q_{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}+i\right)=\frac{11\pi^{2}}{96}-\frac{5L_{-4}(2)}{4}$ in the penultimate column of Table 1, we reach (2.76). $\blacksquare$
It might be a little counterintuitive that the generalizations of (1.5)–(1.8) [see Theorem 1.1(b)] are more sophisticated than those of (1.9)–(1.12) [see Theorem 1.1(a)]. As shown in the proof above, the ‘‘magic cancellations’’ of all the special Dirichlet $L$ -values in the final forms of (1.5)–(1.8) may have obscured the actual complexity of Theorem 1.1(b).
$\square$
