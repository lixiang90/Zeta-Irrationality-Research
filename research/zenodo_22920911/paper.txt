                         ζ(7) is irrational

                                       Abstract

      We prove that ζ(7) is irrational. The proof constructs integer polynomials Qn of degree 37n
      with
                                 0 < Qn(ζ(7)) < exp(︁ −19000 n2)︁

       for all sufficiently large n. These polynomials are rationally normalized Hankel determinants
      attached to a positive moment functional. A positive integral representation gives nonvanishing;
     a logarithmic-energy estimate with respect to an explicit comparison measure gives the real
     bound; local p-adic determinant estimates over Zp[X] control the coefficients after an explicit
      rational normalization. A perturbation estimate also gives |ζ(7)−a/b| > b−270 for every integer
     a and all sufficiently large positive integers b.

1  Introduction

Ap´ery proved in 1978 that ζ(3) is irrational [1]. For higher odd arguments, much of the subsequent
progress has concerned families of values.  Rivoal [11] and Ball–Rivoal [2] proved that infinitely
many odd zeta values are irrational. Zudilin [13] showed that at least one of ζ(5), ζ(7), ζ(9), ζ(11)
is irrational. Later work has strengthened the lower bounds for the number of irrational values [7]
and for the dimension of their rational span [6]. These theorems give information about the family
as a whole, but do not determine the irrationality of any specified odd zeta value beyond ζ(3).
   For ζ(5) itself, Brown and Zudilin [4] constructed an effective infinite sequence of rational
approximations satisfying 0 < |ζ(5) −p/q| < q−0.86.  Their construction uses cellular integrals
and the arithmetic of the associated linear forms. The exponent 0.86 does not by itself imply
irrationality. To obtain irrationality from approximations of this kind, one needs a decay estimate

that remains sufficient after the denominators have been cleared. The same comparison between
real decay and denominator growth governs the polynomial construction used below.
    Recently, Fauzan [5] proved that ζ(5) is irrational by a Hankel-determinant construction with a
positive moment representation and a logarithmic-energy comparison. The present paper extends
that argument to ζ(7). The two changes in the construction are D6N →D8N in the numerator of
the entries of the Gram matrix, and the pole functional
                   j4(X −H(5)j  ) −1 + 1 −→j6(X −H(7)j  ) + 1 −1                                  4   2j                      2j   6.

The rest of the argument follows the same architecture: a positive moment functional, a Hankel-
type determinant, a positive integral representation, a logarithmic-energy comparison, local p-adic
estimates, and a rational normalization that makes the determinant integral.

1.1  The main theorem

Theorem 1.1. The number ζ(7) is irrational.

   The proof uses integer polynomials of growing degree. For each integer M ∈40Z>0 and each
K ∈40Z>0 with K ≥200M2, we construct a rational functional µX, an h × h Hankel-type matrix
GK(X) with h = 37K/40, and a polynomial

                          QK,M(X) = mK,MFK(X),

where FK = SK∆K  is a normalized determinant and mK,M  is a rational normalization factor
defined in Section 5.

Theorem 1.2. For each integer M ∈40Z>0 and each K ∈40Z>0 with K ≥200M 2, the polynomial
QK,M belongs to Z[X], has degree h, and satisfies QK,M(ζ(7)) > 0. For all sufficiently large positive
integers n,

                         0 < Q40n,200(ζ(7)) < exp(︁ −19000 n2)︁ ,                             (1)
                         0 < Q40n,100000(ζ(7)) < exp(︁ −19000 n2)︁ .                           (2)

   The choice M = 200 suffices for Theorem 1.1. The larger cutoff provides the additional decay
used in the approximation bound of Corollary 1.3. The proof of Theorem 1.2 is given in Section B.

1.3  The construction and the proof

The polynomial estimates of Theorem 1.2 imply Theorem 1.1 directly. Indeed, suppose ζ(7) = a/b
with a ∈Z and b ∈Z>0. Then b37nQ40n,200(a/b) is a positive integer. On the other hand, (1) gives

                     0 < b37nQ40n,200(a/b) < exp(︁ 37n log b −19000n2)︁ ,

and the right-hand side tends to zero as n →∞. This is impossible for an integer. Hence ζ(7) /∈Q.
  We now describe the construction behind QK,M. The starting point is a Q-linear functional
µX on rational functions with simple poles among the negative integer squares {−j2  : j ≥1}.
Its values on monomials te are the Bernoulli numbers divided by the appropriate factorial, and
its values on simple poles 1/(t + j2) are affine in X, of the form j6(X −H(7)j  ) + 1/(2j) −1/6.
At X = ζ(7), the functional becomes integration against a positive weight w7(y). This positive
integral representation is the source of the nonvanishing of the determinant.
   Apply µX to the rational functions DN(t)8ti+j/DK(t) with Dm(t) = ∏︁mj=1(t + j2), and form
the h × h matrix
                                                                   [︃   (︃DN(t)8ti+j )︃]︃
                    GK(X) = µX                                     .
                                       DK(t)      0≤i,j<h

Each entry is affine in X, so ∆K(X) = det GK(X) is a polynomial in X of degree h. The matrix
GK(ζ(7)) is a positive definite Gram matrix, so ∆K(ζ(7)) > 0.
  Two distinct estimates complete the proof. First, an arithmetic estimate controls every coeffi-
cient of ∆K(X). This is done by reducing to p-adic completions at each prime, extending µX to a
completed functional τXext on the Tate algebra, and bounding the local determinant valuations in
a suitable basis. The normalization factor SK (a product of factorials) and the additional factor
mK,M (a product of prime powers) clear all denominators and make the polynomial integral.
   Second, a real estimate controls the size of FK(ζ(7)) = SK∆K(ζ(7)). By Andr´eief’s identity,
∆K(ζ(7)) is a multiple integral over h variables, with a squared Vandermonde factor. After scaling,
the integrand is the exponential of a discrete logarithmic energy associated with an external field
V (t). Comparing this energy to the equilibrium measure ρ of mass λ = 37/40, we obtain the
required real bound on the K2 scale.
   Both estimates are balanced against the normalization factor mK,M, and the final inequality

                          lim sup K−2 log QK,M(ζ(7)) ≤AM + U < 0
                 K→∞

follows from the explicit constants AM (from the prime sum) and U (from the real bound) computed
in Sections 5 and 6. The negative quadratic decay then yields Theorem 1.2.
   For Corollary 1.3, smallness of QK,M(ζ(7)) alone is not enough, since the polynomial could
vanish at a nearby rational point. We bound the coefficient matrix of X relative to the positive
definite matrix GK(ζ(7)). This preserves positivity under the required perturbation and permits
the integer argument to be applied at the rational point. The larger fixed cutoff M = 100000
provides the additional decay for that argument.
